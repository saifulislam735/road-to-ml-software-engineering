01

The Mental Model

Four pictures govern every decision in this document. If a choice ever feels confusing, return here — one of these four models resolves it.

1.1 — A call is a streaming pipeline. Latency is the product.

Strip everything away and the system is one duplex loop. A caller expects a reply within ~2 seconds of finishing a sentence; slower than that, they say "hello? hello?", the transcript corrupts, and the call fails. So the latency budget below is the spec — any component that breaks it is disqualified no matter how cheap, accurate, or sovereign it is.

TOTAL BUDGET: caller hears the first word of a reply in <1.2s, full perceived response <2s. Every stage must STREAM — batch anything and the budget dies.

1.2 — The ownership ladder: each rung de-risks the next

Tier 1 · Weeks 1–6
Tenant

Rent everything (~$0.15/min). You are paying a premium to buy the one thing money can't otherwise buy fast: real caller data and a validated automation rate.

Tier 2 · Months 2–4
Homeowner

Own the brain — orchestrator, prompts, intents, integrations, state. Rent the commodities as APIs (~$0.05/min). This brain is permanent: it survives into Tier 3 unchanged.

Tier 3 · Months 5–8
Landlord

Own the ground. Telephony, STT, TTS, and eventually the LLM run on your VPS (<$0.02/call). No foreign data processor touches a single byte. White-label margin becomes real.

The one rule that makes this possibleThe brain never talks to vendors directly. It talks to five interfaces you define — TelephonyAdapter · STTAdapter · LLMAdapter · TTSAdapter · ActionAdapters. Tier 2 plugs rented services behind them; Tier 3 plugs your own services behind them. Migration = swapping adapters, never rewriting logic. This "adapter spine" is the single most important architectural decision in the entire project.

1.3 — The automation funnel: route, don't be a hero

Tier A · ~55%

Fully deterministic. Order status, tracking, FAQ, OTP-gated reads. AI resolves end-to-end, every time.

Tier B · ~25%

Conditionally automatable. Cancellations, returns, address change, refund status. AI resolves when policy conditions hold; escalates cleanly when they don't.

Tier C · ~20%

Never automated. Fraud, legal threats, extreme distress, VIPs, repeated failure. The AI's only job: detect fast, hand off warm, with full context. Human

75% automation = all of A + most of B. But your reputation is made in C: one badly handled fraud call costs more trust than a thousand smooth tracking lookups earn. Escalation is a first-class feature, not a failure mode.

1.4 — Product, not project: the second-tenant test

Every design decision passes one question: "Would this work for tenant #2 without code changes?" Persona name, courier list, business hours, escalation thresholds, policies — all of it lives in per-tenant configuration from Tier 2 day one. You're allowed exactly one tenant in the database for six months. But the column must exist.

02

Ideation → Selection

Five credible strategies were on the table. Four are rejected for specific, structural reasons — not preference. Knowing why not is what keeps you from second-guessing mid-build.

REJECTED
A — Straight to self-hosted

FreeSWITCH + self-hosted Whisper from day one. Maximum sovereignty immediately.

Why not: 4–6 months dark with no revenue, no caller data, no baseline — while fighting three hard problems at once (real-time telephony, Bengali ASR on noisy 8kHz audio, TTS naturalness). The VPS may not even have the GPU this needs. Right destination, wrong starting point.

REJECTED
B — Straight to Tier 2

Skip the SaaS phase; build the orchestrator on rented APIs immediately. Saves 6 weeks of premium fees.

Why not: The orchestrator is the easy 30%. The hard 70% is knowing what it should do — real intents, real phrasings, real failure points. Without Tier 1 data you build that from imagination and rebuild it in production. Tier 1's ~$300–500 premium is the cheapest research you'll ever buy.

REJECTED
C — SaaS forever

Stay on Retell permanently; focus on prompts and selling.

Why not: Three ceilings. Margin: ~$13K+/yr per client leaks to the platform, forever, ×every tenant. Market: data transiting US servers closes the door on banks and telecom (your highest-value verticals). Defensibility: an agency reselling Retell can be displaced by anyone with a Retell account.

REJECTED
D — Chat-first

WhatsApp/Messenger AI first; voice later. 10× easier technically.

Why not: The stated pain is the call center, and BD customers who call are disproportionately the ones who don't self-serve in chat. Chat deflects maybe 15–25% of call volume; the client needs 75%. Build chat later — the Tier 2 brain is channel-agnostic, so chat becomes a thin new transport, nearly free.

SELECTED
E — The three-tier ladder

Live in week 1 on rented SaaS → permanent brain on rented APIs → owned ground, with parallel-run cutovers between each.

Why: The only path that is simultaneously live immediately, informed by real data before the big build, economically viable at scale, regulator-compatible at the end, and free of any full-rebuild step. The sequence is the strategy.

The decisions, with why / why not

Topology: one stateful monolith Owned

WHY — a call is a long-lived, latency-critical, stateful session. One Node process holds the media WebSockets, the per-call state machine, tool execution, and escalation. 50 concurrent calls ≈ 50 WebSocket pairs — one decent VM. Scale later by running N instances with calls pinned per instance; no architectural change needed.

WHY NOT microservices — splitting STT-handling / dialog / tools into services inserts network hops inside the 2-second budget and forces externalizing per-utterance state. Why not serverless: cold starts and execution limits are fatal for persistent duplex streams. The only things split out: the outbound-campaign worker (BullMQ) and the dashboard backend — different load profiles, no real-time audio.

State: memory → Redis → Postgres

WHY — live state in process memory (fastest hot path), snapshot to Redis after each turn (lets the escalation context packet survive a hiccup, feeds post-mortems), append every event to Postgres asynchronously (the durable truth your analytics and tenant billing run on).

WHY NOT Redis-only — serializing on every token taxes the hot path to enable a "crash recovery" that doesn't really exist for phone calls — if the orchestrator dies mid-call, the honest behavior is the call drops and the caller redials. Why not Mongo: your analytics are relational (intents × outcomes × tenants × time) and Postgres row-level security is the multi-tenant substrate later.

STT: Deepgram Rented — eliminated in Tier 3

WHY — purpose-built streaming with sub-300ms finalization, telephony-audio models, Bengali support, interim results that make barge-in responsive.

WHY NOT Whisper API — no true streaming; disqualified by the latency table regardless of accuracy. Why not Google/Azure STT: credible — they enter the bake-off — but Deepgram's latency profile makes it the default. Non-negotiable: the vendor is picked by a measured WER comparison on YOUR 50 real recordings, not by any benchmark on clean audio.

LLM: Claude (streaming + tools) Rented — last to be eliminated

WHY — this system is mostly tool orchestration with a voice; an LLM that hallucinates tool arguments corrupts order mutations. Tool-calling discipline + native Bengali-English code-switching + first-token latency inside the 500ms budget. Run first-utterance intent classification as a separate cheap, constrained call so routing never waits on the dialog model.

WHY NOT self-hosted yet — GPU procurement, serving ops, and open-weight models' weaker Bengali + tool discipline would have you debugging model quality during the phase whose job is validating the business. The self-host path exists (Section 06) — it's sequenced last on purpose.

TTS: decided by blind bake-off, not by brand Rented — eliminated last among media

WHY a bake-off — the voice is the product's face; a mispronouncing voice destroys trust in 5 seconds. Azure ships actual Bengali (bn-BD) neural voices and is cheap; ElevenLabs is the naturalness leader but thin on Bengali; Google sits between. Test 20 real code-switched sentences ("আপনার order টা shipped হয়ে গেছে") blind with 5 native speakers.

WHY NOT default to ElevenLabs — code-switched Bengali is exactly its weak case. Decision deadline: end of build week 2, before flows are voiced.

Escalation: conference-bridge warm transfer Human

WHY — push the context packet (summary, intent, CRM record, sentiment, suggested resolution) to the dashboard → dial the agent → brief them on a private leg → join the caller → drop the AI leg. You control timing and recording continuity. This is the feature clients demo to their bosses — over-invest here.

WHY NOT blind transfer (SIP REFER) — the caller repeats everything from scratch, which is precisely the experience this product exists to kill.

The decisions, with why / why not

Telephony: FreeSWITCH — but spike Jambonz first

WHY FreeSWITCH — thousands of concurrent calls per 8-core box, a threading model built for media, and ESL outbound + mod_audio_fork, which forks call audio to your orchestrator over WebSocket while keeping media threads isolated — one slow LLM response can never jitter audio on fifty other calls. THE SHORTCUT: Jambonz is an open-source, self-hostable CPaaS built on FreeSWITCH that speaks exactly the media-over-WebSocket interface your Tier 2 adapter already uses. One week, hard timebox, to decide. If it fits, months of raw ESL work disappear.

WHY NOT Asterisk — friendlier to learn, but a lower concurrency ceiling and a dialplan-centric model that fights you at white-label scale. Why not Kamailio alone: it's a SIP proxy (signaling only, no media) — relevant later as a load-balancing front to multiple FreeSWITCH boxes, not as the engine.

STT: faster-whisper on a GPU — be honest about the hardware

WHY — Whisper Large-v3 via faster-whisper on a single rented RTX-class GPU (~$100–200/mo) serves dozens of concurrent streams in real time — far cheaper than Deepgram at your volume, and audio never leaves your control.

WHY NOT CPU Whisper — Large-v3 on CPU is >2s/segment — disqualified by the latency table. Fallbacks in order: Distil-Whisper / faster-whisper-medium on CPU (viable latency, but MEASURE Bengali WER degradation first), or deliberately keep Deepgram one more quarter. Keeping a rented organ an extra quarter is a feature of the adapter spine, not a failure.

TTS: Piper with a trained Bengali voice — and a licensing landmine

WHY Piper — fast, CPU-viable, genuinely real-time, permissively licensed. No production Bengali voice exists off the shelf, so you train one: collect/license ~5–10 hours of clean Bengali speech, fine-tune. Run it as a background workstream; cut over only when it beats the cloud voice in blind tests with native speakers.

WHY NOT Coqui XTTS — the company shut down and the XTTS weights carry the CPML non-commercial license — poison for a white-label commercial product. Banned by policy. TTS may legitimately be the last rented organ replaced: a half-good self-hosted voice damages the brand more than a rented one costs.

07

Security Guidelines

Six layers, outside-in. These are not Tier-3 luxuries — every rule below is implemented in the Tier 2 hardening pass (build week 13) and carried forward. A voice agent that can mutate orders is a fraud target from day one; design for the attacker who phones in, not just the one who hacks in.

Layer 1 — Transport everything in motion is encrypted
  • TLS 1.3 for all API traffic — orchestrator ↔ STT/LLM/TTS/OMS/couriers/gateways. No exceptions, no downgrade.
  • SIP-TLS for signaling and SRTP for voice media wherever the trunk supports it; make SRTP support a selection criterion when choosing the IPTSP.
  • Private networking between orchestrator, Redis, Postgres, and (Tier 3) FreeSWITCH — never exposed on public interfaces; SSH key-only access; firewall default-deny.
Layer 2 — Identity the caller is unverified until proven otherwise
  • Never trust caller ID. The calling number selects the likely account for convenience; it never authorizes anything — caller ID is spoofable.
  • OTP gates every mutation. Cancel, return, address change, payment action: OTP to the registered mobile first. Reads of non-sensitive status may proceed unverified; mutations never.
  • Three strikes → fraud flag. 3 failed OTPs ends automated handling and escalates to a human with a fraud flag on the context packet.
  • Rate limiting: max 5 calls per calling number per hour; automated blocking of suspicious dial patterns (sequential numbers, burst dialing).
Layer 3 — Data minimize, redact, encrypt, expire
  • PII redaction BEFORE storage. The async logging pipeline masks phone numbers, NID numbers, and card numbers before any transcript touches disk. Audit this with a real scan — never assume it.
  • AES-256 at rest for call recordings; per-tenant encryption keys from the multi-tenant phase onward.
  • Retention enforced by jobs, not policy documents: recordings auto-deleted at 90 days, transcripts at 1 year, by a scheduled cleanup job that is itself monitored.
  • Postgres row-level security by tenant ID — isolation is enforced by the database, not by remembering a WHERE clause.
  • Data residency: from Tier 2 onward, store all call data in-country where feasible; Tier 3 makes this absolute. Bangladesh Bank / BTRC expectations for finance and telecom clients assume it.
Layer 4 — The LLM boundary caller speech is data, never instructions
  • Structural separation: transcribed caller speech enters the prompt only inside a clearly delimited data field; system instructions are never concatenated with it as peers.
  • Injection stripping: sanitize transcripts for patterns resembling system instructions ("ignore previous…", role-play framing, tool-syntax fragments) before the LLM sees them.
  • Tool allowlist per state: the LLM can only call tools exposed for the current session state — mutation tools are not merely "refused" pre-OTP, they are absent from the tool list. The model cannot call what it cannot see.
  • Constrained outputs: tool arguments validated against strict schemas server-side; the orchestrator — never the model — is the final authority on whether an action executes.
  • Policy comes from RAG, never improvisation: the AI quotes the knowledge base for refund/return policy; if retrieval finds nothing, it escalates rather than guesses.
Layer 5 — Infrastructure privilege the bot is the least-trusted employee
  • Read-only by default: the orchestrator's DB credentials read orders; they cannot write to them.
  • Mutations via dedicated service endpoints only — narrow, audited APIs (cancel-order, update-address) that themselves re-check OTP status and policy windows server-side. Even a fully compromised LLM cannot exceed these endpoints' power.
  • Secrets in environment/secret-store, never in code or prompts; rotated on any personnel or vendor change.
  • Audit log for every action: lookup, cancel, return, transfer — each with timestamp, hashed caller number, session ID, and outcome. Append-only.
Layer 6 — Operations degraded, never dead — and watched
  • Failure mode is human, not silence: if the AI stack is down or latency breaches threshold, inbound calls forward straight to human agents automatically. The phone line never goes dead because your server did.
  • Alerting on: latency p95 breach, OTP-failure spikes (fraud signal), escalation-rate spikes (quality signal), disk/queue saturation, cleanup-job failure.
  • Anomaly review weekly alongside the call-quality ritual; pen test (or structured self-assessment) before Phase 3 exit and before each new vertical (especially finance).
  • Outbound compliance: calling-hour windows, opt-out honored permanently, retry caps — enforced in the campaign engine, not left to configuration discipline.
Non-negotiables — never, under any tier or deadline
  • Never execute a mutation without a verified OTP in the current session.
  • Never authenticate by caller ID alone.
  • Never store an unredacted transcript or an unencrypted recording.
  • Never expose mutation tools to an unverified LLM session.
  • Never let the AI improvise policy it cannot cite from the knowledge base.
  • Never ship Coqui-XTTS-derived voices in the commercial product.
  • Never let the phone line die when the AI dies — fail to humans, always.
